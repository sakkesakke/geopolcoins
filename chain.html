How does a blockchain work? Try it yourself.

No theory first: type, mine and break a tiny blockchain right here in your browser. Nothing is sent anywhere and no real coins are involved.

1. A hash: the fingerprint of data

The SHA-256 fingerprint below changes completely if you change even one letter. You cannot guess the text back from the fingerprint.

…

2. Blocks linked by fingerprints

Each block stores its data, a number called the nonce, and the fingerprint of the block before it. A block is "mined" when its fingerprint starts with 000. Change data in block 2 and watch blocks 2, 3 and 4 turn red. Then press Mine to repair them one by one.

3. Why nobody can secretly change it

  1. Every block points to the one before. Changing an old block changes its fingerprint, which breaks every block after it.
  2. Repairing costs work. Each broken block must be mined again. Here it takes a second; on a real network it would take enormous computing power or stake.
  3. Thousands of copies. Real blockchains are copied on thousands of computers. A faked copy does not match the others and is ignored.
  4. That is why you can check, not trust. Tools like the Token Checkup read this public data so you can verify things yourself.

This is a simplified teaching model. Solana, which $MAIGA runs on, uses proof of stake and proof of history instead of this kind of mining, but the idea of chained, verifiable data is the same.
